## Principal's amendment P-1 — the ingest ceiling

> *"Data & Infra ingests only up to each dataset's holdout cutoff. Holdout periods
> are fetched and locked at Gate 1, by Validation, with my passphrase supplied
> then — never stored."*

This replaces the Charter's fetch-then-encrypt holdout with an **air gap**. It is
strictly stronger and the firm should say why:

- Under the Charter regime, holdout plaintext exists in the ingest process and is
  protected by encryption. Under P-1 it is never fetched, so there is nothing to
  leak. The protection stops depending on the discipline of the code path.
- Because the holdout is fetched at Gate 1 on date **G** rather than at
  pre-registration on date **C**, the window is `[C, G]` — which **contains data
  that did not exist when the hypothesis was written**. That is unseeable, not
  merely unseen, and it is a stronger out-of-sample test than the Charter requires.

### P-1 breaks the harness contract — blocking, resolved before ingest

`HoldoutVault.lock(df, passphrase)` requires the full series in hand: it splits off
the most recent 25%, encrypts the payload, and returns in-sample. `open_once()`
decrypts that stored payload. **Under P-1 there is no payload at lock time.**

Proposed resolution — **Validation rules on this, it is their vault** (Seat 3):

| At | What happens |
|---|---|
| Pre-registration | Vault seals a **holdout specification**, not data: dataset, source, exact query, and the pinned cutoff date `C`. Spec hash written to the registry. |
| Gate 1 | Principal's passphrase authorizes the **fetch**, not a decrypt. Validation pulls `[C, G]`, seals the fetched series, and `open_once()` semantics apply to that fetch. A second fetch raises `HoldoutRetiredError` and is logged as a violation, exactly as today. |

The passphrase's job changes from *gating decryption* to *gating acquisition*.
Single-use enforcement and the registry event log are unaffected.

### Open question routed to Validation, not to the Principal

**Is `C` pinned as a calendar date at pre-registration, or recomputed as "most
recent 25%" at Gate 1?** It must be the former or the in-sample set silently grows
between pre-registration and Gate 1. Recommendation: **pin `C` as an immutable
calendar date in the registry at pre-registration.** Validation's call under
Charter Seat 3; ruling required before Data & Infra's first fetch.

## Named admissibility risk — the Principal's own hypothesis may fail Gate 0 on data length

Raised at intake, per Charter §4.3, because the cheapest stage to fail at is this one.

Charter §4.4 requires backtest length **≥ 4 years AND ≥ 1 full regime cycle**, and a
holdout window of **≥ 12 months**. The forward-lag family's binding leg is
Polymarket, whose history the Charter itself describes as *thin* (§3.2). If usable
contract history is under four years, the family **cannot pass Gate 1 at any
Sharpe** — not because the edge is absent but because the evidence cannot reach the
required length.

Three outcomes, and Validation picks one at Gate 0:

1. **ADMITTED** — usable history clears four years and the 25% holdout clears twelve months.
2. **ADMITTED-AS-EXPLORATORY** — researched and written up, pre-declared ineligible for Gate 1. Honest, and still produces a Research Memo.
3. **REJECTED** — inadmissible at intake.

Note that P-1 partially helps here: because the holdout is `[C, G]` and grows with
elapsed calendar time, a family that is short today can become admissible by
waiting. That is a legitimate use of time, and it is the *only* legitimate one —
the in-sample set does not grow with it.

**Measurement, not assumption:** Data & Infra reports actual usable Polymarket
history as its first deliverable, before Pod B spends a unit on design.
