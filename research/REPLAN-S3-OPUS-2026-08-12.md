## 2 · Two moves that free two units

## 5 · CIO recommendation

**Adopt moves 1 and 2. Run rows 1–4. And rule the slip in advance, now, in one line:**

> **If F-002 survives, or if C2 returns findings requiring a further revision, objective 3 slips to Sprint 4 and the sprint closes on objectives 1 and 2.**

**Why rule it now rather than at close.** Sprint 2's seal slip was ruled in advance and cost the firm nothing but a date; **the sprint that discovers its shortfall at close has to choose between an honest report and a funded one, under time pressure, with the answer already sunk.** Ruling it now removes that choice before it can be faced.

**What the CIO is not proposing, and why.** Not raising the ceiling — *a ceiling that moves when it binds is not a ceiling*, and it is the CIO's own doctrine at reissue. Not drawing on insurance — it is reserved against a failure mode with a measured 27% base rate and **three occurrences in Sprint 2.** Not thinning C2 or C3 — **the adversarial checks are the last thing to yield, and under the Principal's own standing rule they do not yield at all.**

**If the Principal prefers objective 3 funded over objective 1 sealed**, the alternative exists and the CIO states it without recommending it: defer the conformance pass (row 2), accept §11.1 stale and the ratification unrecorded in-field, and hold the freed unit for Gate 1 evaluation. **That trades a known documentary defect for a chance at a verdict, and this firm has spent two sprints establishing that documentary defects are how families die.**
