## 6 · CIO recommendation on the order's disposition

**Recommendation: AMEND AND REISSUE for a second calibration sprint. Do not graduate.**

### Why not graduate

§7 requires **(a) a clean or explained audit and (b) a Charter Part IX amendment at the Quarterly Review.** The audit has not happened — it follows this report — so **(a) is unsatisfied by construction, and any recommendation to graduate would be asking the Principal to ratify a control before its first evaluation.** That alone settles it.

There is a second reason and it is stronger. **The order's §1 was missed on two of four objectives, and the firm did not reach a single terminal verdict.** Graduating a delegation protocol on the strength of a sprint that produced no verdicts would graduate it on process quality alone — which is precisely the substitution §1 was written to forbid: *"the goal is verdicts, not survivals."*

### Why not simple reissue

The order worked, and where it failed it failed in identifiable places rather than diffusely. Reissuing it unchanged would carry four defects the sprint measured:

1. **§2's failure accounting has no headroom clause.** Three terminations consumed 42% of the Opus tier and the order provides no mechanism except spending. The mid-sprint mitigations — pre-split at ~800 lines, analysis/authoring split, Principal-supplied headroom — were ruled in flight and belong **in** the order, not beside it.
2. **§4's interrupt set does not distinguish findings requiring an act from findings requiring visibility.** Eleven interrupts fired; several requested nothing. The Principal settled this in flight — *"§4.4 buys visibility, not decisions"* — and that sentence should be in §4.
3. **§7's audit surface is the decision record and the `[would-have-asked]` log, and I-094 established the record is not harvestable.** The order's own audit mechanism has a retrieval gap.
4. **The order expires with the sprint, which means every durable rule it contains is lost on schedule.** Three were rescued this sprint by hand into `GATES.md` §4.7 and `TEMPLATES.md` §7.9. **A fourth reissue without a promotion path repeats the rescue.**

### What amendment should carry

The three mid-sprint rulings that already function as amendments — the **estimator-change asymmetry** (§8), the **`[PRINCIPAL]`-step verification convention**, and the **headroom/staleness protocol** — plus an explicit **promotion path** for doctrine that should outlive the order.

### The honest case against my own recommendation

A second calibration sprint defers graduation by a full sprint at a firm whose forward-test ledger reads NIL and whose Σα is 0.000. **The strongest argument for graduating now is that the delegation protocol is not what is failing** — the firm's output problem is that no hypothesis has been registered, and no amount of calibration on the delegation layer touches that. **A Principal who concluded that the order is adequate and the research pipeline is the constraint would not be reasoning badly**, and the CIO records that reading rather than arguing only its own.

**What would change my recommendation:** an audit in which few or none of the sixteen would have been decided differently. That would evidence the delegation is calibrated, and the case for a second calibration sprint would rest on the four defects above — repairable by amendment at the Quarterly Review rather than requiring another sprint of observation.
