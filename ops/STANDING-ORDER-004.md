## §3 · Delegated by default

Unchanged from SO-003 §3. The CIO exercises every authority the Charter grants without checkpointing — dispatch and sequencing, intra-budget reallocation, incident response, worktree and merge mechanics, agenda adjustments within the sprint goal.

**§3.1 · Out-of-scope work.** Under §1, **work not on the verdict's critical path is out of scope by default**, may not be funded under §3, and may not be absorbed into a dispatch scoped for something else.

**§3.2 · THE TAG IS MECHANICAL [NEW — Principal-adopted 2026-09-17, origin I-393].**

> **Every decision-record entry carries a `[would-have-asked]` line OR an explicit `[no-discretion]` line. An untagged entry is MALFORMED, and the close report lists any it finds.**

**Origin.** Sprint 4 carried **zero tags across 34 dispatches** [measured], so §7's audit surface existed for nine. **Exhortation failed twice** — Sprint 2 hit this and repaired it in the same entry; Sprint 4 did not notice for a sprint. **Silence previously read identically to "no discretionary decisions were taken," and in Sprint 4 it did not mean that.** The check is mechanical for the same reason §7.10(6)'s read-back is: **a rule that can be satisfied by remembering will eventually not be.**

**§3.3 · Reconstruction is prohibited.** Tags are written by the seat that took the decision, at the time. **A rationale nobody held may not be authored later from the record's prose** — §7.12 applied to the firm's own calibration evidence. **Sprint 4's 25 untagged dispatches are recorded as an audit gap and are not reconstructed.** A fabricated audit surface is worse than an absent one, because it grades clean.
