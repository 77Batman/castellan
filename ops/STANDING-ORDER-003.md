## §3 · Delegated by default

The CIO exercises every authority the Charter grants without checkpointing the Principal — dispatch and sequencing, intra-budget reallocation, incident response, worktree/merge mechanics, agenda adjustments within the sprint goal — logging each decision as it is made and tagging **`[would-have-asked]`** on any decision it would previously have brought to the Principal.

**§3.1 · Out-of-scope work.** Under §1, **document work not on the trial's critical path is out of scope by default.** The CIO **may not fund it under §3** and **may not fund it by absorbing it into a dispatch scoped for something else.** If the CIO believes an out-of-scope item genuinely blocks the trial, **it names it and stops.**

---

## §4 · Hard interrupts — enumerated, mechanical, no judgment

Unchanged. The loop halts and queues on any of: any Charter §2 reserved authority · any seal, kill-condition signature or restatement, or KC silence-kill firing · **any issue filed HIGH** · any finding by Validation, the CRO or the Devil's Advocate addressed to the Principal · any drawdown-ladder rung · any action requiring the holdout passphrase · any detected Charter–harness divergence · budget exhaustion in either tier, including the §2 insurance.

**Silence is never approval for an interrupt item.**

**§4.1 · Act versus visibility.** **§4 buys visibility, not decisions.** A finding that requests no act **still interrupts**, and **the CIO may not decline to queue an item on the ground that it appears to need no ruling** — that assessment is the Principal's, and making it is the narrowing §8 forbids.

## §5 · Dead-man clause

**10 invocations or 5 calendar days** since the last Principal checkpoint, whichever first, halts everything. **A halted firm is a correct outcome; an unsupervised one is not.** The count runs from the most recent checkpoint and **is restated accurately in every CIO report — neither stale nor conservative.**

## §6 · Checkpoint cadence

The Principal reviews the **Friday Research Review**, the **Monthly Letter**, and the **interrupt queue as it arrives.** The decision record, the `[would-have-asked]` log and the **findings index** are the review surface between checkpoints.

**The Friday `[PRINCIPAL]` ritual carries four items**, each pasted to the record per `TEMPLATES.md` §7.9: **pull-and-merge** · **dated-clause review** · **I-095 gap review** (until the registry/vault read-only fix lands — bound to *before first trials log*) · **version-floor verification.** **A skipped week is a skipped verification and is logged as such.**

## §7 · Calibration audit, findings index, and graduation

At sprint close the Principal audits every `[would-have-asked]` entry.

**§7.1 · The findings index is Sprint 4's opening act** and is objective 1's third dependency. **I-098 is its fifth motivating instance and the most dangerous:** the Red Team's ten findings — including the one blocking the seal — **were written into the memo and never into the log**, so a seal proceeding on the CIO's report rather than on the memo would have shown **no seal-blocking issue at the moment of sealing.**

**§7.2 · Graduation** requires a clean or explained audit and a Charter Part IX amendment at the Quarterly Review. **Sprint 3's verdicts were to be the evidence; there were none.** **The CIO does not propose graduation and will not until the firm has logged a trial.**

**§7.3 · Sunset and promotion.** This order expires with the sprint. **Every durable rule born inside it is assigned a permanent home at close, as a standing mechanism.** *(Executed for SO-002 on 2026-08-25 → `GATES.md` §4.7.5, `TEMPLATES.md` §7.10.)*
